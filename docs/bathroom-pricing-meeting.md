# Bathroom quote pricing meeting

Meeting date: October 21, 2026

This worksheet records how the bathroom questionnaire should price each quote section and every number the team still needs to decide.

**Status:** Sections 1–7 have an agreed pricing method, but their dollar amounts are TBD. Sections 8–19 are proposals to decide at the meeting. None of the price-book defaults below are live in the app yet.

## Pricing types

- **Manual allowance:** The questionnaire adds the quote row and description, but an estimator enters the customer price, subcontractor cost, and material cost.
- **Standard price — editable:** The questionnaire calculates a default from the company price book. An estimator can replace the suggested amount for an unusual job.

## Decisions that apply to every section

For each checked price below, record the **customer price, subcontractor/labor cost, material cost, unit, minimum charge, what is included, rate owner, and last reviewed date**. Use $0 for a cost component only when it truly does not apply. An unanswered selection or missing default must be flagged for estimator review rather than silently producing a finished $0 quote.

- [ ] How are customer prices set from sub and material costs: fixed customer rate, markup, target margin, or a mix?
- [ ] Does material cost include sales tax, delivery, and waste? Are permit fees marked up?
- [ ] Should a trade minimum apply once per bathroom per trade, rather than once per item?
- [ ] What tile waste percentage applies to standard and patterned layouts? How are square feet rounded?
- [ ] Are customer-supplied products assigned $0 material cost while installation stays priced?
- [ ] When an estimator overrides the suggested price, may they also override projected sub and material costs?
- [ ] What quote validity, deposit/payment schedule, exclusions, and change-order terms apply to every quote?

## Questionnaire data needed for reliable autofill

- [ ] Numeric room length, width, and ceiling height with units
- [ ] Numeric shower width, depth, and actual tile height with units
- [ ] Which shower walls are tiled, including windows, doors, half walls, and custom shapes
- [ ] Measured bathroom floor area to tile, including treatment under the vanity or tub
- [ ] Tile type, size, pattern, and supplier for shower walls, shower floor, and bathroom floor separately
- [ ] Vanity width and sink count; counts of toilets, lights, outlets, switches, and fans
- [ ] Shower base, drain, glass configuration, fixture selections, and any relocated plumbing/electrical
- [ ] Known damage, unusual substrate, difficult access, and permit requirements

Candidate calculations: shower floor = width × depth; shower walls = sum of tiled wall widths × tile height; tile material area = installed area × (1 + approved waste percentage). An estimator reviews calculated quantities before the quote is sent.

## 1. Site protection, demolition, and disposal

**Decision:** Manual allowance.

- Add one consolidated quote row.
- Build its description from the selected demolition work.
- Do not calculate packages or square-foot pricing yet.
- Estimator enters the customer price, subcontractor cost, and material/disposal cost.

## 2. Framing, drywall, and wall repairs

**Decision:** Triggered manual allowance.

Add one consolidated row when the questionnaire identifies any of the following:

- Divider wall removal or modification
- Shower footprint changes
- Framing for a niche, bench, glass, or accessories
- Drywall, ceiling, subfloor, or other wall repairs

Omit the row when none of those conditions apply. The estimator enters the customer price, subcontractor cost, and material cost.

## 3. Plumbing

**Decision:** Standard price — editable.

- Calculate an internal default from the selected plumbing work.
- Combine the calculation into one customer-facing Plumbing row.
- Allow the estimator to replace the calculated total.
- Keep drain, supply-line, and fixture relocation as a manual allowance.

### Default prices needed at the meeting

- [ ] Shower valve and fixture replacement
- [ ] Vanity sink and faucet connection, per sink
- [ ] Toilet installation or reset
- [ ] Tub installation
- [ ] Plumber minimum charge

For each default, decide the customer price, subcontractor cost, material cost, and what the standard price includes.

## 4. Electrical and ventilation

- Calculate an internal default from the selected electrical and ventilation work.
- Combine the calculation into one customer-facing Electrical and ventilation row.
- Allow the estimator to replace the calculated total.

### Default prices needed at the meeting

- [ ] Recessed or shower light installation, per fixture
- [ ] GFCI outlet work, per outlet
- [ ] Switch or dimmer work, per switch
- [ ] Exhaust-fan replacement

For each default, decide the customer price, subcontractor cost, material cost, and what the standard price includes.

## 5. Shower base and waterproofing

**Decision:** Standard price — editable.

- Combine the calculation into one customer-facing Shower base and waterproofing row.
- Calculate the default from the shower dimensions, base type, and selected features.
- Include the flood test in the standard price.
- Keep structural repairs and discovered water damage in the manual Framing, drywall, and wall repairs allowance.

### Default prices needed at the meeting

- [ ] Standard curbed tiled shower base
- [ ] Curbless shower upgrade
- [ ] Prefabricated shower base installation
- [ ] Shower wall waterproofing, per square foot
- [ ] Shower floor waterproofing, per square foot
- [ ] Niche waterproofing, per niche
- [ ] Bench waterproofing, per bench

For each default, decide the customer price, subcontractor cost, material cost, minimum charge, and what the standard price includes.

## 6. Shower wall tile

**Decision:** Standard price per square foot — editable.

- Calculate the wall area from the back-wall width, both side-wall depths, and tile height.
- Include normal layout, cutting, thinset, grout, and installation labor in the standard rate.
- Keep tile purchasing in the separate Tile material allowance section.
- Add selected complexity charges to the calculated default.
- Allow the estimator to replace the calculated total.

### Default prices needed at the meeting

- [ ] Standard shower-wall tile installation, per square foot
- [ ] Large-format tile upgrade, per square foot
- [ ] Mosaic or small-tile upgrade, per square foot
- [ ] Herringbone or complex-pattern upgrade, per square foot
- [ ] Natural-stone upgrade, per square foot
- [ ] Niche tile installation, per niche
- [ ] Bench tile installation, per bench
- [ ] Edge trim or specialty profile, per linear foot
- [ ] Minimum shower-wall tile charge

For each default, decide the customer price, subcontractor cost, included setting materials, and what qualifies for each complexity upgrade.

## 7. Shower floor tile

**Decision:** Standard price — editable, with a fixed base price and square-foot overage.

- Calculate shower-floor area from the shower width and depth.
- Cover an agreed floor size with one standard installation price.
- Add a per-square-foot charge only above that size.
- Add selected upgrades for pebble, irregular mosaic, natural stone, or a complex drain layout.
- Keep tile purchasing in the separate Tile material allowance section.
- Show one customer-facing Shower floor tile row and allow the estimator to replace the calculated total.

### Default prices needed at the meeting

- [ ] Standard shower-floor tile installation price
- [ ] Floor area included in the standard price, in square feet
- [ ] Overage rate per square foot
- [ ] Pebble or irregular-mosaic upgrade
- [ ] Natural-stone upgrade
- [ ] Complex drain-layout upgrade

For each default, decide the customer price, subcontractor cost, included setting materials, and which drain layouts count as complex.

## Sections proposed for discussion

The methods in sections 8–19 are suggestions, **not approvals**. Confirm or change each one before assigning its rates.

## 8. Bathroom floor tile

**Proposed method:** One editable installation price per square foot, with a minimum for small bathrooms. Tile purchasing stays in section 10.

### Default prices to decide

- [ ] Standard bathroom-floor tile installation, per square foot
- [ ] Minimum installation price per bathroom
- [ ] Large-format, mosaic, natural-stone, or complex-pattern upgrades: decide which are needed and their rates

**Decision needed:** Does normal underlayment belong in this rate? Which patterns need an add-on?

## 9. Floor preparation and underlayment

**Proposed method:** Include normal preparation and standard underlayment in section 8. Add one **manual allowance** only for substantial leveling, damaged subfloor, concrete work, or unusual substrate conditions.

**Decision needed:** Define normal preparation and the conditions that trigger the manual row. If the team chooses a separate standard prep rate, remove it from section 8 to avoid double counting.

## 10. Tile material allowances

**Proposed method:** One editable allowance internally calculated from shower-wall, shower-floor, and bathroom-floor tile quantities, each with its own selected material rate and the approved waste percentage. Customer-supplied tile has a $0 purchasing allowance while installation remains priced.

### Default prices to decide

- [ ] Shower-wall tile material, per purchased square foot or by tier
- [ ] Shower-floor tile material, per purchased square foot or by tier
- [ ] Bathroom-floor tile material, per purchased square foot or by tier
- [ ] Standard waste percentage and patterned-layout waste percentage
- [ ] Sales tax and delivery rule

**Decision needed:** Show one combined material allowance or separate allowances by tile selection? Do not count thinset, grout, or other setting materials here if an installation rate already includes them.

## 11. Shower features

**Proposed method:** One editable allowance for selected niches, benches, shelves, grab bars, and special accessories. Include only work and materials not already priced in framing, waterproofing, tile, plumbing, or shower fixtures.

### Default prices to decide

- [ ] Recessed niche finish or accessory, per niche
- [ ] Bench finish or accessory, per bench
- [ ] Corner shelf, per shelf
- [ ] Grab bar and installation, per bar
- [ ] Custom feature, manual allowance

**Decision needed:** Would a separate feature row help the customer, or should each feature stay inside its trade row? Handheld and rain-head products belong in section 12.

## 12. Shower fixtures

**Proposed method:** One editable **material allowance** for the chosen valve, trim, shower head, handheld, rain head, diverter, and finish. Installation stays in Plumbing.

### Default prices to decide

- [ ] Standard valve, trim, and shower-head material package
- [ ] Handheld material add-on
- [ ] Rain-head material add-on
- [ ] Diverter/additional outlet material add-on
- [ ] Premium finish/brand upgrade or custom product allowance

**Decision needed:** Use standard product tiers or actual selected-product cost plus markup? Valve material must appear here or in Plumbing, only once.

## 13. Shower glass

**Proposed method:** One editable allowance based on enclosure configuration. Unusual or oversized glass requires a vendor quote after field measurement.

### Default prices to decide

- [ ] Fixed panel, standard material and installation
- [ ] Door and panel, standard material and installation
- [ ] Frameless enclosure, standard material and installation
- [ ] Specialty glass or hardware upgrade
- [ ] Glass vendor minimum, if applicable

**Decision needed:** Which configurations are safe to price from a default? Glass blocking and wall repairs belong in section 2.

## 14. Vanity, countertop, sink, and faucet

**Proposed method:** One editable package based on vanity size and sink count. Include cabinet and countertop work plus product allowances. Plumbing connections remain in section 3; electrical work remains in section 4.

### Default prices to decide

- [ ] Double-vanity or added-width installation upgrade
- [ ] Floating/custom-vanity installation upgrade
- [ ] Vanity/cabinet material, by width or selected tier
- [ ] Countertop material and fabrication, per square foot or package
- [ ] Sink material, per sink

**Decision needed:** Use fixed size tiers or measured vanity width? Clarify delivery, sink cutouts, and backsplash inclusion.

## 15. Toilet

**Proposed method:** An editable toilet **material allowance** when replacement is selected. Toilet installation/reset remains in Plumbing; relocation remains a manual plumbing allowance.

### Default prices to decide

- [ ] Standard toilet material, per toilet
- [ ] Premium/specialty toilet material upgrade, per toilet

**Decision needed:** Show the allowance in its own row or within Plumbing? Customer-supplied toilets have $0 purchasing allowance.

## 16. Mirror, lighting, fan, and accessories

**Proposed method:** One editable **finish-material allowance** for selected products. Electrical installation stays in section 4. Count specialty mounting or carpentry exactly once.

### Default prices to decide

- [ ] Mirror or medicine cabinet material, per unit
- [ ] Vanity-light material, per fixture
- [ ] Exhaust-fan material, per fan, unless included in the electrician rate
- [ ] Towel bar, hook, toilet-paper holder, and similar accessories, per item or package

**Decision needed:** Which products does Premium Remodel normally supply? Is fan material already included in section 4?

## 17. Paint, trim, caulking, and final cleanup

**Proposed method:** One editable standard finish package per bathroom. Add a manual or measured increase for extensive trim or repainting. Demolition cleanup remains in section 1; final finish cleaning belongs here.

### Default prices to decide

- [ ] Standard bathroom paint, trim touch-up, caulking, and final cleanup
- [ ] Full-room repaint upgrade
- [ ] New baseboard or trim installation, per linear foot, if needed

**Decision needed:** Is painting always included? Define the threshold for a trim add-on.

## 18. Permits and inspections

**Proposed method:** One manual allowance only when required. Use a confirmed local fee when known; otherwise flag the amount for review.

**Decision needed:** Which jobs usually require permits, who verifies the fee, and is permit-handling labor included elsewhere? A universal default is unnecessary unless the team has a reliable local fee schedule.

## 19. Other work and unforeseen conditions

**Proposed method:** A manual row for requested work outside the standard bathroom packages. Treat hidden damage as a clearly labeled allowance or exclusion subject to change-order terms, rather than pretending the repair is fully priced.

**Decision needed:** Does the team want an optional contingency percentage, and should it appear on the customer quote? What work belongs in the initial quote versus a later change order?
